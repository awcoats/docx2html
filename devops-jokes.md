Since you've already survived the first round of deployment, here is a second batch of DevOps humor, categorized by your specific flavor of professional pain.

The "YAML & Configuration" Tier
*   How many DevOps engineers does it take to change a lightbulb?
    None. They just update the lightbulb.yaml to define the current state as darkness: true and call it a feature.
*   Why did the DevOps engineer break up with their partner?
    Because there was too much configuration drift and they couldn't agree on a single source of truth.
*   A DevOps engineer walks into a bar.
    They order a drink, but then spend the next three hours trying to figure out why the bar's API is returning a 418 I'm a teapot error.

The "Kubernetes & Containers" Tier
*   Why did the Kubernetes Pod go to therapy?
    It was having an identity crisis and couldn't figure out which node it belonged to.
*   What is a DevOps engineer's favorite horror movie?
    The Silence of the Lambdas.
*   Why don't Docker containers ever get lost?
    Because they always stay within their defined boundaries. (Unless you forgot to set a memory limit).
*   An optimist says, "The glass is half full."
    A pessimist says, "The glass is half empty."
    A DevOps engineer says, "The glass is currently running in a container that is behaving as expected, but we should check the logs to see if it's actually leaking."

The "Monitoring & On-Call" Tier
*   What’s the difference between a developer and a DevOps engineer?
    A developer thinks "it works on my machine" is a valid defense. A DevOps engineer knows "it works in my cluster" is a lie that will cost them their sleep at 3:00 AM.
*   What is the most common lie told in a Post-Mortem?
    "We didn't see any alerts for this."
*   Definition of "Observability":
    The ability to watch your entire infrastructure fail in real-time with high-resolution graphs that tell you exactly when you died, but not why.

The "CI/CD" Tier
*   Why do DevOps engineers prefer CI/CD pipelines over humans?
    Because when a pipeline fails, it gives you a clear error message. When a human fails, they just say, "It worked on my local branch."
*   A developer says, "I've finished the feature!"
    The DevOps engineer says, "Great, now let's see how much we can break the build with it."
*   Why was the CI/CD pipeline so tired?
    Because it was constantly under continuous pressure.

And finally, the DevOps Mantra:
"If it takes more than five minutes to fix, it wasn't a bug; it was an undocumented architectural decision."

The three stages of a production deploy: confidence, denial, rollback.
